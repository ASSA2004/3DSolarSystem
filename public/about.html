A solar system for the classroom.

Orbit helps students in Classes 6–8 explore the Sun and planets. Teachers can lead short activities. Students can look closely, compare measurements and check their understanding.

There are no accounts, advertisements or analytics. Quiz scores and preferences stay on the device. The app can be used offline once it confirms that its offline copy is ready.

What the model can teach

Normal view lets you move around planets. Details view explains rotation, orbits, dates and distances. Video view provides a guided tour. Teacher lessons use questions before explanations, giving students time to think and discuss.

Be clear about scale and accuracy

The overview enlarges planets and compresses distances. True scale uses physical distances and planetary diameters; labels help you find tiny objects. Planet positions use JPL's approximate J2000 elements for 1800–2050. Earth represents the Earth–Moon centre of mass. Spins and moon positions are illustrative. This is not a navigation tool or a precise map of the sky.
